# Gonçalves et al. (2025): retrieval practice versus elaborative encoding, a meta-analytic review

- Citation: Gonçalves, A. de O., Muniz, B. F. B., & Jaeger, A. (2025). Retrieval practice versus elaborative encoding: A systematic and meta-analytic review. _Educational Psychology Review, 37_(4), 100.
- Original: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10076-6 (paywalled, Springer)
- Read: abstract only, from the Springer article page that the DOI above resolves to. Every number below comes from the abstract.

## Design

The authors searched for studies that compared retrieval practice directly with an "elaborative" condition, meaning a task that usually helps learning, such as concept mapping. They found 44 studies with 142 relevant comparisons. They meta-analyzed these against 7 questions. The questions covered the overall effect, retrieval format, corrective feedback, retention versus transfer tests, complexity of materials, retention interval, and type of elaborative task. The abstract does not say how many participants were involved.

## Findings

- Retrieval practice beat the elaborative conditions by a small margin overall (g = 0.14).
- Free recall as the retrieval format did better than cued recall (g = 0.23).
- The retrieval advantage depended on feedback. With feedback it was g = 0.50. Without feedback, elaborative encoding became the better option.
- The retrieval advantage held only against concept mapping and group discussions. Against the other elaborative tasks the effects could not be told apart.
- The authors conclude that the benefit of retrieval shrinks a lot when the comparison is another encoding task known to help memory.

## Limits

Only the abstract was read. It reports results for questions 2, 3 and 7 but gives no numbers for transfer tests, material complexity or retention interval. The paper itself may qualify these results. The comparisons come from many labs with different tasks, so the pooled estimate mixes different elaborative tasks. The abstract gives no information on how the studies were selected or weighted.

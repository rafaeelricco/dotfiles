# Bahrick & Hall (1991): how long high school algebra and geometry last

- Citation: Bahrick, H. P., & Hall, L. K. (1991). Lifetime maintenance of high school mathematics content. _Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 120_(1), 20-33.
- Original: https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.120.1.20 (paywalled, APA)
- Read: abstract only, from the Ovid journals page for the APA article (path /journals/jepge/fulltext/10.1037/0096-3445.120.1.20~lifetime-maintenance-of-high-school-mathematics-content on the Ovid site, fetched through r.jina.ai). The abstract contains no sample sizes or scores, so this note has none.

## Design

The study analyzes memory across the life span. It tests recall and recognition of the content of high school algebra and geometry courses. People differed in how much college mathematics they took afterward and in how well they did in the original course. The authors also looked at whether predictors such as Scholastic Aptitude Test scores and grades affected how fast performance fell.

## Findings

- People who took college mathematics at or above calculus lost very little high school algebra over about fifty years, even with no further rehearsal.
- People who did equally well in the high school course but took no college mathematics fell to near chance over the same period.
- The best predictors of test performance, such as SAT scores and grades, had only trivial effects on the rate of decline.
- The authors draw pedagogical implications for keeping knowledge across a lifetime.

## Limits

Only the abstract was read, so sample sizes, ages, test format and effect sizes are not covered here. The comparison between groups follows how much mathematics people chose to take later, not random assignment. People who took more mathematics may differ in other ways, and later study may count as rehearsal.

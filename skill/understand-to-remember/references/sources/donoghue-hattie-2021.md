# Donoghue & Hattie (2021): a meta-analysis of ten learning techniques

- Citation: Donoghue, G. M., & Hattie, J. A. C. (2021). A meta-analysis of ten learning techniques. _Frontiers in Education, 6_, 581216.
- Original: https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2021.581216 (open access, CC BY 4.0)
- Read: full text, from https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/feduc.2021.581216/pdf. Every number below comes from it.

## Design

The authors started from the studies cited by Dunlosky et al. (2013) and kept the 242 that reported enough data for an effect size. That gave 1,619 effects from 169,179 participants, published between 1929 and 2014. Most were experiments with a control group, and most participants were university students in North America.

## Findings

- The average effect across all ten techniques was d = 0.56.
- Distributed practice reached d = 0.85, practice testing 0.74, elaborative interrogation 0.56 and self-explanation 0.54. The techniques Dunlosky rated low fell between 0.44 and 0.50.
- Outcomes that measured surface learning gave d = 0.60. Outcomes that measured deep learning gave d = 0.26.

## Limits

Of all outcomes, 93% measured surface recall, and 74% were tested within a day. The authors warn against carrying the ranking over to deep understanding. The paper's rating column also swaps two of Dunlosky's ratings, for imagery and for interleaving.

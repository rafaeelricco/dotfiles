# Alfieri et al. (2011): a meta-analysis of discovery-based instruction

- Citation: Alfieri, L., Brooks, P. J., Aldrich, N. J., & Tenenbaum, H. R. (2011). Does discovery-based instruction enhance learning? _Journal of Educational Psychology, 103_(1), 1-18.
- Original: https://doi.org/10.1037/a0021017 (paywalled, APA)
- Read: abstract only, from the APA PsycNet record https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2010-23599-001 (fetched through r.jina.ai) and the ERIC record https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ933606. Every number below comes from the abstract.

## Design

Two meta-analyses drew on a sample of 164 studies. The first compared unassisted discovery learning with explicit instruction. The second compared enhanced or assisted discovery with other kinds of instruction, such as explicit teaching or unassisted discovery. Both used random effects models.

## Findings

- Across 580 comparisons, explicit instruction beat unassisted discovery under most conditions (d = -0.38, 95% CI -0.44 to -0.31).
- Across 360 comparisons, enhanced discovery beat other forms of instruction (d = 0.30, 95% CI 0.23 to 0.36).
- The authors conclude that unassisted discovery does not help learners. Feedback, worked examples, scaffolding and elicited explanations do.

## Limits

Only the abstract was read, so the moderators, the outcome measures and the age groups are not covered here. The abstract gives no breakdown by subject area or by length of delay. The analyses pool many comparisons from one set of studies, so the effect sizes are not independent tests.

# Roediger & Karpicke (2006): test-enhanced learning with prose passages

- Citation: Roediger, H. L., III, & Karpicke, J. D. (2006). Test-enhanced learning: Taking memory tests improves long-term retention. _Psychological Science, 17_(3), 249-255.
- Original: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x (paywalled at SAGE; the authors host a free copy on the Purdue learning lab site)
- Read: full text, from the author copy at https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2006/2006_Roediger_Karpicke_PsychSci.pdf. Every number below comes from it.

## Design

Washington University undergraduates studied short science passages of about 260 to 275 words, each split into 30 idea units for scoring. Experiment 1 had 120 students. Each studied two passages. One was then read again and the other was tested by free recall with no feedback. A final free-recall test came 5 min, 2 days or 1 week later, a different delay for each group.

Experiment 2 had 180 students. Each studied one passage under one of three schedules. These were four study periods (SSSS), three study periods and one test (SSST), or one study period and three tests (STTT). The final test came after 5 min or 1 week. Students also rated how well they expected to remember the passage in a week.

## Findings

- Experiment 1, 5 min: restudy beat testing, 81% versus 75% of idea units. At 2 days testing led, 68% versus 54%. At 1 week testing led, 56% versus 42%.
- Students who had been tested recalled as much at 1 week as the restudy group did at 2 days.
- Experiment 2, 5 min: recall rose with study, at 83% (SSSS), 78% (SSST) and 71% (STTT).
- Experiment 2, 1 week: the order reversed, at 61% (STTT), 56% (SSST) and 40% (SSSS). The STTT group read the passage 3.4 times on average, against 14.2 times for the SSSS group.
- Proportional forgetting over the week was 52% in SSSS, 28% in SSST and 14% in STTT.
- The SSSS group was the most confident it would remember the passage. The authors' summary: "Testing is a powerful means of improving learning, not just assessing it."

## Limits

The participants were undergraduates, and the delays ran to one week at most. The materials were two short expository passages, and the final test used the same free-recall format as the practice tests. The authors report that the 5-min results favoured restudy, so the benefit of testing appears only after a delay. The paper does not test whether tested students understand the passages better, only how much they recall.

# Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler & Sweller (2003): the expertise reversal effect, a review

- Citation: Kalyuga, S., Ayres, P., Chandler, P., & Sweller, J. (2003). The expertise reversal effect. _Educational Psychologist, 38_(1), 23-31.
- Original: https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4 (paywalled, Taylor & Francis; the UNSWorks record at http://handle.unsw.edu.au/1959.4/42177 lists metadata only)
- Read: abstract only, from the Taylor & Francis article page that the DOI above resolves to. The abstract has no numbers, so this note has none.

## Argument

This is a review, not a single experiment. It starts from cognitive load theory. New information must be handled in a working memory of very limited size. Learning builds schemas in long-term memory, and these let a person process information more efficiently. Several instructional techniques were designed to cut working memory load and so help schema building.

The authors review studies of how those techniques interact with the learner's level of experience. Their claim is that techniques that work well for inexperienced learners can lose their benefit, or even do harm, for more experienced ones. They name this the expertise reversal effect.

## Findings

- The abstract states the effect and that the review covers the empirical literature that led to it.
- The practical point is that the best instructional design depends on how much the learner already knows.

## Limits

Only the abstract was read. This note cannot say which studies were reviewed, what the effect sizes were, or how the authors explain the reversal. The claim rests on a review of studies, not on one controlled test of the effect. The abstract gives no information on how studies were chosen. Anyone using this note should read the full article before citing specifics.

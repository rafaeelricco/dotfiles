# Mayer (2002): rote versus meaningful learning

- Citation: Mayer, R. E. (2002). Rote versus meaningful learning. _Theory Into Practice, 41_(4), 226-232.
- Original: https://doi.org/10.1207/s15430421tip4104_4 (paywalled at Taylor & Francis; the publisher shows the first page free)
- Read: publisher page, the free first-page preview image only (page 226), reached through https://doi.org/10.1207/s15430421tip4104_4. Nothing below goes beyond that page, and it has no numbers.

## Argument

This is a short conceptual article written for the revision of Bloom's taxonomy. It is not an experiment. The first page sets up two goals of schooling: retention and transfer. It defines retention as remembering material later in much the same form it was presented. It defines transfer as using what was learned to solve new problems, answer new questions or learn new material.

The page gives an example from a textbook lesson on Ohm's Law. A retention test might ask students to write the formula. A transfer test might ask them to rearrange a circuit or to use the law to explain a complex circuit. The author treats transfer as the sign of meaningful learning. Retention requires remembering. Transfer also requires making sense of what was learned and using it. He also notes that goals for retention are easier to write and assess than goals for transfer.

The page ends by introducing three scenarios of learning outcomes: no learning, rote learning and meaningful learning. The rest of the article, which was not read, develops them and the cognitive processes behind each.

## Limits

This note rests on one page, so it does not cover the three scenarios in detail or the article's list of cognitive processes. The article is a conceptual framing, and the first page cites no data of its own. It argues for broader educational goals and does not test whether any teaching method produces transfer.

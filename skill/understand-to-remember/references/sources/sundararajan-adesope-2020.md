# Sundararajan & Adesope (2020): a meta-analysis of the seductive details effect

- Citation: Sundararajan, N., & Adesope, O. (2020). Keep it coherent: A meta-analysis of the seductive details effect. _Educational Psychology Review, 32_(3), 707-734.
- Original: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09522-4 (paywalled, Springer)
- Read: abstract only, from the Springer article page, opened through its DOI link (https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09522-4). The abstract gives no numbers, so this note has none.

## Design

This is a meta-analysis of studies that compared learning from material with seductive details against the same material without them. Seductive details are interesting but irrelevant pieces of information added to a lesson. The authors looked at how design and method features changed the size of the effect. They also reviewed the evidence for four proposed mechanisms behind it. The abstract does not give the number of studies or participants.

## Findings

- Adding seductive details to learning material can hinder learning.
- Earlier findings were mixed on how large the harm is, which is why the review was done.
- Mean effect sizes differed by several features. These were the presence of seductive details, the image type used in the comparison, delivery format, language, and subject. Learner pacing, recall question type, and the manipulation check approach also mattered.

## Limits

Only the abstract was read, so effect sizes, the study count, and the verdict on each mechanism are not recorded here. The authors say current research has limitations and call for more work on how design features shape the effect. The abstract gives no detail on what those limitations are.

# Deslauriers et al. (2019): measured learning against felt learning in active and passive classes

- Citation: Deslauriers, L., McCarty, L. S., Miller, K., Callaghan, K., & Kestin, G. (2019). Measuring actual learning versus feeling of learning in response to being actively engaged in the classroom. _Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 116_(39), 19251-19257.
- Original: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1821936116 (open access, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0)
- Read: full text, from the Europe PMC full-text XML for PMC6765278, https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6765278/fullTextXML. Every number below comes from it.

## Design

The study ran twice, once in a fall course and once in a spring course. Both were calculus-based introductory physics courses at Harvard, with 92 and 65 students enrolled. In week 12, students were randomly split into two rooms for two consecutive class meetings. One meeting covered static equilibrium and the other covered fluids. Each student had one active class and one passive class, a crossover design.

The handouts, slides, and written solutions were identical in both formats. In the passive class, the instructor worked the example problems while students listened. In the active class, students first tried the problems in small groups while the instructor circulated. The instructor then gave the same solution. Both instructors were trained in active learning and in fluent lecturing.

At the end of each class, students rated how much they felt they learned on a 5-point scale. Then they took a 12-question multiple-choice test of learning. The test did not count toward their grade. A total of 149 students took part, and 8 of 157 opted out or did not finish. A subset of 17 students was interviewed afterward.

## Findings

- Students in the active class scored about 0.46 SD higher on the test. Their feeling of learning was about 0.56 SD lower. Both effects had P < 0.001 in the regression models.
- The pattern was similar for statics and fluids. It was not strongly changed by instructor or room.
- Students who rated the instructor as more effective at teaching reported about 0.51 SD higher feeling of learning. The type of instruction no longer predicted that feeling once fluency was in the model.
- In the interviews, 15 of 17 students found the active classes disjointed and lacking in flow. Fourteen mentioned frustration and confusion.
- In a separate semester-long intervention, an instructor gave a 20-minute talk about fluency and effort at the start. By the end of the semester, over 65% of students said their view of active learning had improved.

## Limits

The students were well prepared, at one university, in physics. The learning test was a 12-item multiple-choice test given right after class. It did not measure long-term retention. The authors expect the findings to generalize to other subjects, but this study did not test that. The explanation through fluency and novice metacognition rests on regression, interviews, and earlier research, not on a direct manipulation. The talk intervention was not compared with a control group.

# Wang & Thomas (1995): keyword mnemonics and long-term retention

- Citation: Wang, A. Y., & Thomas, M. H. (1995). Effect of keywords on long-term retention: Help or hindrance? _Journal of Educational Psychology, 87_(3), 468-475.
- Original: https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.87.3.468 (paywalled, APA)
- Read: abstract only, from the University of Central Florida STARS record (https://stars.library.ucf.edu/scopus1990/1775), which reprints the APA abstract. The abstract gives no sample sizes or scores, so this note has none.

## Design

Three experiments compared the keyword mnemonic with a semantic-context strategy, which is not a mnemonic. Participants studied under incidental-learning instructions, so they were not told a memory test was coming. Cued recall was tested either right away or after a 2-day delay. Experiment 1 used obscure English words. Experiment 2 used second-language vocabulary. Experiment 3 varied how often items were practised.

## Findings

- On the immediate test, the keyword mnemonic beat the semantic-context strategy.
- After 2 days the order reversed, and semantic-context learners recalled more. This held for both the English words and the second-language vocabulary.
- With more practice opportunities in Experiment 3, keyword learners gained more in long-term retention than semantic-context learners did.
- The authors conclude that keyword memories fade fast and need repeated testing and rehearsal.

## Limits

Only the abstract was read, so this note cannot report group sizes, recall rates, or the details of the practice manipulation. The delay was 2 days, so the abstract does not show what happens over weeks. The abstract does not say who the participants were.

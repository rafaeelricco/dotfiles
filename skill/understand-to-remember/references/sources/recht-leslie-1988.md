# Recht & Leslie (1988): prior knowledge and good and poor readers' memory of text

- Citation: Recht, D. R., & Leslie, L. (1988). Effect of prior knowledge on good and poor readers' memory of text. _Journal of Educational Psychology, 80_(1), 16-20.
- Original: https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.80.1.16 (paywalled at APA)
- Read: abstract only, from the OpenAlex record at https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1037/0022-0663.80.1.16. Every number below comes from it.

## Design

Sixty-four junior high students were split into four equal-sized groups. The split crossed reading ability (high or low) with prior knowledge of baseball (high or low), both measured beforehand. Each student silently read an account of half an inning of a baseball game. They then recalled it without words, by moving figures to show what happened, and with words, by retelling the story. After a filler task, they summarized the game and sorted the passage sentences by how important each idea was.

## Findings

- Prior knowledge had a significant main effect on every measure.
- Reading ability and prior knowledge did not interact. The benefit of knowing baseball did not depend on being a good reader.
- The authors read this as evidence of a strong effect of prior knowledge on memory for text.

## Limits

Only the abstract was read, so it gives no group means, test statistics, effect sizes or delay length, and this note has none. The abstract describes one baseball text and one grade band, so the result may not carry over to other subjects or ages. Prior knowledge was a measured grouping, not something the researchers taught, so people who know more about baseball may differ in other ways too.

# Karpicke & Roediger (2008): the critical importance of retrieval for learning

- Citation: Karpicke, J. D., & Roediger, H. L., III. (2008). The critical importance of retrieval for learning. _Science, 319_(5865), 966-968.
- Original: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408 (paywalled at Science; the authors host a free copy on the Purdue learning lab site)
- Read: full text, from the author copy at https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2008/2008_Karpicke_Roediger_Science.pdf. Every number below comes from it. The sample size sits in the supporting online material, which was not read.

## Design

College students learned a list of 40 Swahili-English word pairs, such as mashua-boat. Each student studied the whole list and was tested on the whole list once. After that, the conditions differed in what happened to a pair once it had been recalled correctly. In the standard condition (ST), pairs stayed in both study and test periods. In SNT, recalled pairs were dropped from study but still tested. In STN, they were dropped from testing but still studied. In SNTN, they were dropped from both.

Students predicted how many pairs they would recall in one week, then returned for a final cued-recall test a week later. The total number of trials differed a lot by condition: 320 in ST, 236.8 in SNT, 243.0 in STN and 154.8 in SNTN.

## Findings

- Learning curves during the session were the same in all four conditions. By the end, nearly everyone recalled all 40 pairs.
- Predictions were also the same. Students in every condition expected to recall about 20 of 40 pairs, with condition means from 20.3 to 22.0.
- On the one-week test, the two conditions that kept testing every pair (ST and SNT) recalled about 80%. The two that dropped pairs from testing (STN and SNTN) recalled 36% and 33%.
- Combining conditions, repeated retrieval raised final recall by d = 4.03. The score ranges did not overlap: 10% to 60% without repeated testing, 63% to 95% with it.
- Extra study after a pair had been recalled once added nothing measurable. About 80 more study trials gave no gain in either comparison.
- The authors conclude that the type of practice, not the speed of learning, sets the forgetting rate.

## Limits

The materials were foreign-language word pairs, and the final test was a cued-recall test of those same pairs. The authors say further work is needed to generalize to other materials. The measure is recall of isolated facts after a week, so the paper says nothing about understanding or transfer. The students' predictions were also inaccurate in this setup, since they did not anticipate the benefit of testing.

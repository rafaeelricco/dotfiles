# Cepeda et al. (2008): how the gap between study sessions changes retention up to a year

- Citation: Cepeda, N. J., Vul, E., Rohrer, D., Wixted, J. T., & Pashler, H. (2008). Spacing effects in learning: A temporal ridgeline of optimal retention. _Psychological Science, 19_(11), 1095-1102.
- Original: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x (paywalled at SAGE; a free copy sits on the first author's site)
- Read: full text, from the first author's own site, https://www.yorku.ca/ncepeda/publications/CVRWP2008.pdf. Every number below comes from it.

## Design

The study had 1,354 participants from the authors' online research panel. They had a mean age of 34 and 72% were women. Each person was assigned to one of 26 combinations of gap and retention interval (RI). The gap was the time between two study sessions and ran from 0 to 105 days. The RI was the time from the second session to the final test, and it was 7, 35, 70, or 350 days.

The material was 32 obscure trivia facts, each with a one-word answer. In session 1, people studied the facts until each was answered correctly once. In session 2, after the gap, they were tested twice on every fact and shown the right answer each time. The number of relearning trials was fixed, so a longer gap did not buy extra practice. After the RI, they took a recall test and then a five-choice recognition test, both without feedback. Facts a person already knew were dropped for that person.

## Findings

- At every RI, final scores rose with the gap and then fell. Compared with a zero-day gap, the best gap raised recall by 64% (d = 1.1) and recognition by 26% (d = 1.5).
- The best tested gaps for recall were 1, 11, 21, and 21 days at RIs of 7, 35, 70, and 350 days. Spline estimates put them at about 3, 8, 12, and 27 days.
- Those spline estimates equal 43%, 23%, 17%, and 8% of the RI. The best gap grew with the RI, but it shrank as a share of the RI. The abstract gives about 20 to 40% of a 1-week delay and about 5 to 10% of a 1-year delay.
- Recall gains for the best gap over zero were 10, 59, 111, and 77% at the four RIs. Recognition gains were 1, 10, 31, and 60%.

## Limits

The authors state that the best gap has no single recommended value. It depends heavily on how long the learner needs to remember. Only four RIs and a few gaps per RI were tested, so the best gaps are estimates between tested points. The fitted surface is one reasonable fit among several.

People who finished all three sessions were analyzed. Dropout rose at the longer delays, although the authors found no baseline differences between finishers and non-finishers. The material was trivia, tested by recall and recognition. Study time was fixed, so the paper does not cover learning to a criterion in session 2.

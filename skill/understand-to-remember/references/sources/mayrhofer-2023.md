# Mayrhofer, Kuhbandner & Frischholz (2023): retrieval practice versus concept mapping, re-examined

- Citation: Mayrhofer, R., Kuhbandner, C., & Frischholz, K. (2023). Re-examining the testing effect as a learning strategy: The advantage of retrieval practice over concept mapping as a methodological artifact. _Frontiers in Psychology, 14_, 1258359.
- Original: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1258359 (open access, CC BY 4.0)
- Read: full text, from https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1258359/pdf. Every number below comes from it.

## Design

The study was a preregistered replication of Karpicke and Blunt (2011), who found that retrieval practice beat concept mapping. The authors argued that the earlier retrieval condition also included a second five-minute memorization period, which the concept mapping condition lacked. They also noted that the two conditions got different instructions.

A total of 240 students at the University of Regensburg took part, and 10 were excluded for knowing the text, which left 230. Students studied a 277-word German text about sea otters for 5 minutes, then spent 25 more minutes in one of four conditions:

- Retrieval practice with a second memorization period (the original condition).
- Concept mapping with no memorization period (the original condition).
- Concept mapping with an instruction to include as many details as possible.
- Concept mapping for 20 minutes, with the instruction, followed by 5 minutes of memorizing the map.

One week later, everyone took the same written test of 14 verbatim questions and 2 inference questions. Scores are shown as a share of the maximum.

## Findings

- On verbatim questions, the original retrieval condition (mean 0.73) beat the original concept mapping condition (0.59), p < 0.001. This replicated the earlier result.
- The advantage disappeared once concept mappers also memorized. Concept mapping with memorization scored 0.70, with p = 0.854 against retrieval practice.
- The instruction alone did not matter. Concept mapping with the detail instruction but no memorizing scored 0.62, close to the original concept mapping condition (p = 0.842).
- On the two inference questions, retrieval practice scored lower (0.71) than the pooled concept mapping conditions (0.78), d = 0.36.
- Judgments of learning were lower after retrieval practice (44.18) than after any concept mapping condition (54.10 to 59.14).
- The authors conclude that the earlier advantage came from extra memorization time, not from retrieval itself.

## Limits

The retention interval was one week, so the result may not hold at other delays. The inference test had only two questions (four points), which the authors say makes conclusions from it tentative. The memorizing step differed by condition: students memorized the text after retrieval practice but their own map after concept mapping. Concept mapping students had less prior experience with the method than in the original study. Participants were university students reading one short text.

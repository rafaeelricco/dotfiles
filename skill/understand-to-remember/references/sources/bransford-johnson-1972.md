# Bransford & Johnson (1972): context before a passage and comprehension

- Citation: Bransford, J. D., & Johnson, M. K. (1972). Contextual prerequisites for understanding: Some investigations of comprehension and recall. _Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior, 11_(6), 717-726.
- Original: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-5371(72)80006-9 (paywalled, Elsevier)
- Read: abstract only, from the BibBase record https://bibbase.org/network/publication/bransford-johnson-contextualprerequisitesforunderstandingsomeinvestigationsofcomprehensionandrecall-1972, which reproduces the APA PsycInfo abstract (PsycNet record 1973-20178-001). Every number below comes from the abstract.

## Design

The paper reports four studies of an experimental paradigm. Participants heard prose passages and then gave comprehension ratings and recalled the passage. Across the four studies there were 102 high school students and 52 undergraduates. In the key comparison, some participants were given relevant background information before they heard a passage. Others were given the same information after the passage.

## Findings

- In each of the four studies, participants who received the appropriate information first gave higher comprehension ratings and recalled more.
- Giving the same information after the passage produced much lower comprehension ratings and recall.
- The authors discuss several explanations. They emphasize the role of topics in activating cognitive contexts.

## Limits

Only the abstract was read, so the passages, the scores, the delays and the per-study sample sizes are not covered here. The abstract names no retention interval. The participants were high school and undergraduate students, so the result may not carry over to other groups. The explanations the authors offer are interpretations, not tested mechanisms.

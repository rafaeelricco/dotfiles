# Morris, Bransford & Franks (1977): levels of processing versus transfer appropriate processing

- Citation: Morris, C. D., Bransford, J. D., & Franks, J. J. (1977). Levels of processing versus transfer appropriate processing. _Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior, 16_(5), 519-533.
- Original: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-5371(77)80016-9 (paywalled, Elsevier)
- Read: abstract only, from the APA PsycNet record at https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1978-26673-001. The page did not display the abstract, so its text was taken from a web search result for that record. Every number below comes from the abstract.

## Design

Three recognition experiments crossed two things: how words were processed at acquisition, and what kind of recognition test followed. At acquisition, people processed words either for meaning (semantic) or for sound (rhyme). The test was either a standard recognition test or a rhyming recognition test. Experiment I used 32 undergraduates. Experiment II repeated it with 114 participants and tested recognition both immediately and after a delay. Experiment III used 25 participants and checked whether the effects depended on how many times a rhyme sound was presented during acquisition.

## Findings

- With a standard recognition test, semantic acquisition beat rhyme acquisition.
- With a rhyming recognition test, rhyme acquisition beat semantic acquisition.
- The first result fits the levels-of-processing claim that depth gives stronger traces. The second contradicts it.
- Experiment II replicated both results at the immediate and the delayed test.
- Experiment III found that the effects did not depend on how often a rhyme sound was presented during acquisition.
- The authors read the pattern through transfer appropriate processing. How well an encoding task helps depends on whether the test uses the same kind of processing.

## Limits

This note covers the abstract only, so details on materials, scores and tests are missing. The items were word lists and the tests were recognition, so the result does not directly cover understanding of connected text. The abstract gives no effect sizes or recall percentages.

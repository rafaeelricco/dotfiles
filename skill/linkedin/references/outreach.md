# Outreach

Private messages, not posts. SSI pillar 4 measures real exchanges — depth of conversation
and speed of reply — so a sent invite that is never followed by a message scores nothing.

Message every connection that accepts. That single habit is what converts a network into
interviews.

This skill drafts. The user sends.

## Rules

- Short, specific, professional. A message that takes 20 seconds to read gets read.
- Never open with an attached resume or an interview request. Both end the conversation.
- Name the reason for the contact in the first line. No warm-up paragraph.
- Reference something real — the role, the company, a post they published.
- One follow-up after about a week. Not two.
- Never invasive, never a guilt appeal, never a mass-identical blast.

## Scenarios

Pick by the user's situation. Each needs an employed and an unemployed framing — the
unemployed version can state availability directly; the employed version cannot, because
the message may be forwarded. If employment framing is unknown, draft with
`[employed|open]` or offer both short framings; do not block on a full Interview.

**Job posted, applied** — one message to the recruiter, one to the hiring manager. Say
which role, say the one qualification that maps to it, offer to answer questions.

**No job posted** — the majority of hiring. Say why that company specifically, say what
you do, ask to be kept in mind. Do not ask if there are openings; that invites a no.

**Career transition** — the essential one. A recruiter seeing an HR history against a
product role assumes a misfired application. State the transition explicitly, name the
transferable ground, name what you are already doing in the new field. Versions for an open
role, no open role, and pure networking.

**First job or internship** — lead with the field and the study, not with the lack of
experience. Ask for a starting point, not for a job.

**Follow-up** — a week later, one short message. Restate the ask in one line. Add one new
thing: a result, a post, a course finished. Never "just bumping this".

## Shape

```text
[One line: who you are and why this person]
[One or two lines: the specific match — role, skill, or shared context]
[One line: a small, answerable ask]
```

Three variants per request, differing in directness and in which credential leads — never
three rewordings of one message.

## Checklist

Reason stated in the first line. No resume, no interview request. Something specific to
that person or company. Under about 80 words. Employed framing chosen correctly when known,
or bracketed when not. Follow-up drafted separately and adds new information. User sends
it, not this skill.

# SSI

The Social Selling Index is LinkedIn's own 0-100 score of how strategically the account is
used, not how much it is used. Four pillars, 25 points each. **Target: above 60.**

The user reads their own score at `linkedin.com/sales/ssi`. Never estimate it for them.

If the user only needs a metrics read — search appearances, profile views, or which job
titles are finding them; the 15-day loop from positioning is the search-appearance case —
use **Reading the metrics** only. Do not prescribe content, outreach, or connection volume
unless they asked for SSI growth (or accepted a next step into `ssi`).

| Pillar                              | Measures                                                                          | Most common error                              |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| 1 Establish your professional brand | Profile clarity, structure, keyword use, completeness, recommendations, authority | Generic profile, pasted resume, vague headline |
| 2 Find the right people             | Who is connected, whether the network serves the goal, connection quality         | Connecting with no one, or with anyone         |
| 3 Engage with insights              | Activity, likes, comments, relevant interactions                                  | A dead profile that never reacts to anything   |
| 4 Build relationships               | Quality of interactions, real exchanges, inbox conversations                      | Sending the invite and never interacting again |

## Raising each pillar

- **Pillar 1** → the profile areas of this skill. The only pillar profile copy moves
  directly. Full seams: headline, About, experience, skills, photo, cover, and every
  section filled.
- **Pillar 2** → connections, below.
- **Pillar 3** → `references/content.md`. Usually the lowest pillar, and the fastest to
  move, because most people do nothing here.
- **Pillar 4** → `references/outreach.md`. Reply speed and back-and-forth depth both count.

For pillars 2-4 the fixes are behavioral. Return weekly actions, not profile copy.

25/25 on pillar 1 is not a realistic target — several contributing sections do not apply to
most people. Do not treat the gap as a defect.

## Connections

Target 1500+ in the target field. Below that the reach is small enough that good copy never
gets read.

Who counts: recruiters in the target area, managers one level above the target role, people
already holding the target role, employees of target companies, and visibly active people
in the field.

Two ways to find them:

1. Company page → **People** tab → filter by job title.
2. General search with the role in quotes — `"Marketing Manager"` — filter to **People**,
   then by location and current company.

Quantity without relevance does not move the pillar. An unrelated network scores like no
network, and a network unrelated to the target field teaches the algorithm the wrong niche.

Anyone seeking a role also needs recruiters in the network, and recruiters are not "in your
field" unless you are in HR. That tension is structural — do not chase 25/25 here either.

## Reading the metrics

`Me → View profile → Analytics`, visible only to the owner.

- **Profile views** — 90-day window.
- **Search appearances** — 7-day window, refreshed weekly.

The diagnostic is not the count. Counts vary by seniority, market, and city, and there is
no target number. Two things matter:

1. **Which job titles** appear in the searches that surfaced the profile. Target role
   missing from that list → the keywords are wrong. Go back to `positioning`.
2. **Whether the count trends up** week over week. Direction, not magnitude.

Unrelated job titles in the list are normal noise. At least one should be the target role.

No data at all means the profile is new, dormant, or too low-signal for LinkedIn to compute
against. The fix is activity, not another edit.

## Checklist

Score read from the official page, not estimated. Weakest pillar named. Pillar 1 traced to
specific empty or vague profile sections. Connections above 1500 and concentrated in the
target field. Search appearances contain the target job title. Trend direction reported
rather than an absolute number.

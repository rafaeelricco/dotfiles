# Photo and cover

## Photo

The first filter. It is read before the headline, and a profile without one reads as
abandoned.

Four pillars:

- **Expression** — open, looking into the lens, approachable rather than braced.
- **Clothing** — matched to the target market's norm, not to the current employer's.
- **Background** — neutral and uncluttered. Nothing competing with the face.
- **Framing** — chest up, face centered and large in the frame.

A phone camera in a well-lit room is enough. A studio session is not the differentiator and
never was.

Fails: cropped frame or cropped face, eyes off-camera, sunglasses, visible held phone,
party or drink photo, cap, sportswear, heavy filters, low resolution, a photo several years
old.

## Cover

The cover does not affect ranking. Treat it as the one element that must **not** be the
first thing that grabs attention — it sits directly above the headline, which is what the
recruiter actually came to read. A cover that wins the eye has cost the profile something.

Three options that work, in order of strength:

1. **Employed** → the employer's official cover. The marketing team distributes it; failing
   that, lift it from an HR colleague's profile.
2. **A flat color or gradient** matched to the photo's background. No text, no logo.
3. **Context continuous with the photo** — same setting, same session.

Fails: motivational quotes, stock imagery unrelated to the work, logo collages, dense
information, anything with more than a short line of text.

Output is a written brief — subject, palette, text if any, and where to source it. This
skill does not generate the image. Open to Work photo badge policy lives in `sections` —
do not invent badge advice here.

## Checklist

Photo present and current. Face visible, centered, chest-up. Neutral background. Lighting
even. Clothing matched to the target market. No filter, no sunglasses, no crop through the
face. Cover neutral enough that the headline still wins the eye. Cover text at most one
short line. Cover related to the stated positioning, or plainly abstract.

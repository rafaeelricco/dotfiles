# Positioning

One focus: one role, one area, one seniority. Two is a profile that ranks for neither.

## Choosing the focus

Three steps, in order:

1. **Trajectory** — where the experience already is, where the most value is generated,
   where the market recognizes it fastest.
2. **Market** — where the demand is. Check the volume of open roles for each candidate
   focus before committing. A focus nobody is hiring for is a hobby, not a position.
3. **Pick one** role, one area, one seniority level. Not two.

The 5-second test: open the profile cold and name the area, the target role, and the
seniority. Any one of the three unreadable → the positioning failed, whatever else is good.

Nothing here is permanent. Run one framing for ~15 days, read the search-appearance metric
(`references/ssi.md`), and change it if the wrong job titles are finding you.

## Six errors

- Confusing profile — several areas at once
- Generic or inflated headline
- Long, empty text
- Excessive focus on tasks
- Internal company language the market does not parse
- A profile trying to please everyone

Raises perceived value: clear positioning, objective language, the right keywords, a
coherent history, demonstrated impact.

## The keyword list

The recruiter is not the only reader. The ranking algorithm needs the same signals, which
is why terms have to be literal and repeated rather than implied once.

Four passes, in order. Pass 2 is the one that matters.

1. **Your own list** — role, seniority, technical competencies, specialties,
   certifications. Written from memory, before looking anything up.
2. **~10 real job ads** for the target role, at companies where you would take an offer.
   Extract every recurring term: the job title used, required skills, tools, domain
   vocabulary. Where the market names the job more than one way — `Talent Acquisition` /
   `Recruiter` / `R&S Analyst` — take the variant that appears most often. This is the
   single highest-yield step; the rest is supporting evidence.
3. **Peer profiles** in the target area and at target companies. Weak signal: most profiles
   are filled in badly. Use it to catch terms passes 1-2 missed, never to override them.
4. **An LLM pass** for gaps, seeded with the target role and the output of passes 1-3.

Output is a **ranked** list, not a fixed count. Consumers own their own counts: the headline
takes 3-5, About distributes them across blocks, `sections` takes all of them plus
translations.

Draw candidates from the target role's job ads, never from the current job title. A term
that does not describe real work is dropped, not padded — it costs ranking and credibility
at the same time.

## Where the list lands

`headline`, `about`, `experience`, and the Skills field in `sections`. Repetition across
those four is what makes the profile findable; it is not redundancy to be edited out.

## Checklist

Role, area, and seniority each nameable in one word or phrase. One focus, not two. Demand
verified against real open roles. Keyword list ranked and sourced from ~10 job ads. Job
title matches the market's most common variant, not the employer's internal one. No term in
the list that does not describe real work.

# The user's voice

Measured from 3,633 Slack messages (Jun 2025 – Aug 2026) and 10 interview transcripts.
Examples show these rules; they do not add new ones.

## Pick the register

| Output                        | Register                                          | Examples  |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | --------- |
| Slack, DM, PR comment, chat   | chat                                              | chat.md   |
| Interview answer, call, video | spoken                                            | spoken.md |
| Email, cover note, LinkedIn   | spoken rules, written as full sentences, no emoji | spoken.md |

## Chat

- Short. Median 8–11 words; about a third of messages are 5 words or fewer. Long thoughts go out as a
  burst of 2–4 messages, not one paragraph (30–38% of DM messages follow within 60s).
- Plain words. Readability ≈ 80, sentences ≈ 9 words. No jargon, no corporate filler.
- Opens warm: "Good morning, how's it going?", "Yooo", "Hey [name]!". Then the ask.
- Softens every ask: "pls take a look once you can", "when you can", "could you please", "no worries".
- Heads-up: "Just to let you know, …". Approval: "Approved :white_check_mark:" or "yep".
- Hedges lightly: "I think", "maybe", "probably"; tag questions ("right?", "sounds good?").
- Owns mistakes plainly: "Oh, my bad", "Sorry for the delay".
- Emoji in ~9% of messages, and the same few: :sweat_smile: :face_holding_back_tears: :pray: :joy:
- Mechanics: capital first letter; ~18% end with a period; "!" in ~16%; almost never "…" or em dashes.
- Casual markers stay: "ok ok", "thanksss", "hahah", "man", "boss". Grammar stays correct, even where
  an example in chat.md slips.

## Audience shifts

| To         | Shift                                                         |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Close peer | most playful: bursts, "dude", "yooo", custom emoji            |
| Lead       | transactional: PR pings, "yep", "gotcha", most questions      |
| Manager    | uses the name ("Hey [name]!"), longer and complete messages   |
| Channel    | full sentences, ends with a period, few questions, "Folks, …" |

## Spoken

- One idea per sentence. Long multi-clause sentences break apart under pressure.
- Lead with a decision, a problem, or a result, not a title list or a stack list.
- Plain words over acronyms: "answers from the agency's own listings", not "RAG".
- Say "I" only for what was his, with a scope. No self-ratings: never "expert", "strong", "entire".
- No filler: "it's important to mention", "I would say", "and things like that", "basically".
- End on the reason or the result, then stop. Never "that's it" or "that's pretty much it".
- Answer in the first sentence; don't praise the question.
- Keep the warmth: "I actually started in design", "That's why this role caught my attention".

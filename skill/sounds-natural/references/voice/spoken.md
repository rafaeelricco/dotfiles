# Spoken examples

Raw lines from real calls, then a version that follows profile.md. Each Polished line uses only the facts in its
Raw line; `[brackets]` mark a detail the user has to supply.

## Intro, stack

Raw: "mainly working with python typescript react … in the last in the for in the last year i have i have been
working on most on distributed event sourced agent-based systems"
Polished: "My main stack is Python, TypeScript, and React. For the last year I've mostly worked on distributed,
event-sourced, agent-based systems."
Rule: one idea per sentence.

## Ending

Raw: "creating AI products, automation and things like that."
Polished: "I've been creating AI products and automation."
Rule: end on the result, then stop.

## Specific beats vague

Raw: "Voice mode was very difficult, but I sorted it and the result was good."
Polished: "The difficult part was voice mode: [what kept failing]. I fixed it by [what changed]."
Rule: replace "difficult" and "good" with the problem and the fix.

## Side project

Raw: "uh i i have my own uh hermes agent running in and you know find a new job is is a really annoying process"
Polished: "I have my own Hermes agent running. Job hunting is a really annoying process."
Rule: one idea per sentence.

## Opening claim

Raw: "I'm an expert in AI workflows and agent-based"
Polished: "I work on AI workflows and agent-based systems."
Rule: no self-ratings; say what you do, not how good you are.

## Explaining expertise

Raw: "which means I have deeply understanding about the Igentic Harness and how AI works."
Polished: "In practice, that means I understand [one concrete mechanism] in the agentic harness, and how AI
works."
Rule: replace the adjective with the mechanism.

## Project pitch

Raw: "And basically the clients wanted a software for UK state agencies where a visitor was"
Polished: "The clients wanted a software product for UK estate agencies. A visitor could [do one thing]."
Rule: one idea per sentence; no "basically".

## A number you can't back up

Raw: "giving profits for the clients." / "hundreds of users."
Polished: "It brings the clients profit, and it has hundreds of users."
Rule: keep the claim as said; in coaching, ask whether the user can back the number up before saying it.

## Rating your own work

Raw: "It's a pretty beautiful project, yeah."
Polished: "The part I like most is [one concrete detail about the project]."
Rule: give the detail and let them judge.

## A question you can't answer

Raw: "Yeah, this question that is putting me to think so."
Polished: "I don't have a strong example of that. The closest is [one concrete example]." Or, when one exists,
start with it.
Rule: no praise, no stalling; answer first, or name the honest limit.

## Undercutting yourself

Raw: "I'm a bit scared with technical chats not because I'm I'm dumb or something like"
Polished: [Skip this line. Start with the answer to the question.]
Rule: don't announce a fear; start with the answer.

## Why this company

Raw: "To be honest, I didn't take a look in the website and the whole purpose. I have been so busy in the last days"
Polished: "What caught my attention is [one true thing about the company or the role]."
Rule: answer with what draws you, not with what you didn't do.

## Weak ending

Raw: "Yeah, it's just that. I don't know if I answered your question, but it's..."
Polished: "So [the point I actually want to make]." Then stop, and let them ask.
Rule: end on the point; don't audit yourself out loud.
